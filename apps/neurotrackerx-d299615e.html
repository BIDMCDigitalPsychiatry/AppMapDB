How apps are evaluated

Rater review

This review represents the view of the app rater and is not an endorsement by MIND.

NeuroTrackerX is a cognitive training application that uses 3D multiple-object tracking (MOT) exercises to train attention, visual processing, and other perceptual-cognitive skills. The platform is grounded in neuroscience research and has been investigated in peer-reviewed studies. One app-specific study evaluated the feasibility of remote NeuroTrackerX training in older adults and reported high adherence and retention. However, this study primarily assessed feasibility and usability rather than demonstrating clinical efficacy or improvements in everyday functioning. Although the broader NeuroTracker technology has been studied in several populations, these findings should not be interpreted as proof that the app effectively treats specific medical conditions. The app provides interactive training exercises, performance assessments, and progress data, with potential applications in cognitive rehabilitation and performance training. Its professional features may be useful for clinicians and researchers, although it is not a substitute for professional medical evaluation or treatment. The app is available on iOS and Android, but its interface is listed in English only. The pricing model may also limit accessibility, as continued individual use requires a paid subscription or lifetime purchase after the free trial. Privacy disclosures also warrant further review. Google Play reports that the app collects and shares no data, whereas Apple’s privacy labels identify categories of data that may be linked to users, and the developer’s privacy policy and terms describe the processing of account, health, exercise, and performance information. This inconsistency makes it important for users to review the applicable privacy documentation before entering sensitive information. Overall, NeuroTrackerX offers a structured cognitive training experience, but its clinical benefits for specific conditions remain insufficiently established by the app-specific evidence identified, and its cost, English-only user interface, and privacy-disclosure discrepancies are specific and notable limitations.
